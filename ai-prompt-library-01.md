# River AI Rangers — AI Prompt Library 01

## Lesson 1: Is Our River Healthy?

These prompts are designed to be used with the River AI Rangers AI assistant at river-ai-rangers.streamlit.app. They are written for secondary school students aged 11–16. Teachers can display them on a board or print this page and cut the cards.

---

## How to Use This Library

- Pupils choose a prompt card and type it (or something like it) into the AI assistant  
- The AI responds in simple language  
- Pupils are then encouraged to ask a follow-up question of their own  
- **Always end with the critical question:** *"How sure are you about that? How could I check?"*

---

## CARD SET A — Understanding the Numbers

**Card A1 — pH**

> "Our river has a pH of \[number\]. Is that healthy for the animals living in it? Please explain using simple words."

**Card A2 — Nitrates**

> "Our river's nitrate level is \[number\] mg/L. What does that mean for the river? Is it safe?"

**Card A3 — Phosphates**

> "Our river has \[number\] mg/L of phosphates. What causes phosphates to get into rivers, and is our level worrying?"

**Card A4 — Overall picture**

> "Our river has a pH of \[number\], nitrates of \[number\] mg/L, and phosphates of \[number\] mg/L. Overall, do you think our river is healthy? Why?"

---

## CARD SET B — What Lives There?

**Card B1 — Wildlife**

> "If a river has these readings — pH \[number\], nitrates \[number\], phosphates \[number\] — what kinds of animals and plants might live in it?"

**Card B2 — Sensitive species**

> "Which animals are most sensitive to pollution in rivers? Would they survive in our river?"

**Card B3 — Algae**

> "What is an algae bloom and why is it bad for rivers? Could it happen in our river?"

**Card B4 — Recovery**

> "If a river is polluted, can it recover? What would need to happen?"

---

## CARD SET C — Where Does Pollution Come From?

**Card C1 — Sources**

> "Where do nitrates and phosphates in rivers usually come from? Can you give me some examples?"

**Card C2 — Local causes**

> "What kinds of things near a town or village could make a river's phosphate levels go up?"

**Card C3 — Farms**

> "How do farms affect river water quality? What is fertiliser run-off?"

**Card C4 — Sewage**

> "What is sewage overflow and how does it affect rivers? Is this a problem in many countries?"

---

## CARD SET D — Taking Action

**Card D1 — Who is responsible?**

> "Who is responsible for keeping rivers clean? What can ordinary people do?"

**Card D2 — Writing to someone**

> "I want to write a letter to my local council about our river's water quality. What should I include?"

**Card D3 — Persuasion**

> "Help me write one sentence that explains why high phosphate levels in rivers are a problem. Make it easy to understand for someone who has never heard of phosphates."

**Card D4 — Citizen science**

> "What is citizen science? How do ordinary people help monitor rivers?"

---

## CARD SET E — Critical Thinking (use with every session)

These prompts teach pupils to question and evaluate the AI's responses. At least one should be used every lesson.

**Card E1**

> "How confident are you about what you just told me? Could any of it be wrong?"

**Card E2**

> "Where does this information come from? How could I check if it's true?"

**Card E3**

> "Is there anything you don't know about this topic that I should find out from a real scientist?"

**Card E4**

> "If a real river scientist read your answer, what might they disagree with or want to add?"

---

## Teacher Notes

**What the AI does well:** explaining concepts, translating numbers into plain language, suggesting questions to investigate, helping with writing structure.

**Where pupils should be cautious:** very local or very recent data (the AI does not know your specific river), precise legal thresholds (check with your river group), and anything that sounds surprisingly certain.

**The golden rule for this classroom:** *The child is the scientist. The AI is the tool.*

---

## Prompt Starters for Pupils Who Are Stuck

If a pupil doesn't know what to ask, use these sentence starters:

- "Can you explain what \_\_\_ means in simple words?"  
- "What would happen to the fish if \_\_\_?"  
- "Is \_\_\_ dangerous for the river? Why?"  
- "What could cause \_\_\_ to happen?"  
- "What should someone do if they found out \_\_\_?"
