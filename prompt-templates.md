# Prompt Templates for Educators
This library of ready-to-use prompts is designed for secondary school teachers using the River AI Rangers toolkit. Copy, paste, and adapt them freely.
All prompts are tested with the **River AI Rangers assistant at [river-ai-rangers.streamlit.app](https://river-ai-rangers.streamlit.app)** (powered by Anthropic Claude — no installation required).

---

## How to use these prompts

A good prompt has three parts:

1. **Context** — what the data is and where it came from
2. **Question** — what you want to know
3. **Audience instruction** — who the answer is for

The templates below are ready to use. Replace `[BRACKETS]` with your actual values.

---

## 🔬 Template 1 — Interpreting a single reading

Use when: a student has one water-quality result and wants to know what it means.

We measured the [pH / nitrate / phosphate] level at a river site near [location or description: e.g. "a farm" / "a town centre" / "a woodland"].
The reading was [VALUE].
Please explain:

Whether this is a healthy or unhealthy level
What it might mean for the fish and invertebrates that live in the river
What might be causing this reading
Please use clear, precise language suitable for a secondary school student.

**Example with values filled in:**

> We measured the nitrate level at a river site downstream from farmland. The reading was 22 mg/L. Please explain whether this is a healthy or unhealthy level, what it might mean for the fish and invertebrates that live in the river, and what might be causing this reading. Please use clear, precise language suitable for a secondary school student.

## 📊 Template 2 — Comparing two or more sites

Use when: students are comparing upstream vs downstream, or seasonal changes.

We measured water quality at [NUMBER] sites along the same river:

Site [A/name]: pH [X], nitrates [X] mg/L, phosphates [X] mg/L
Site [B/name]: pH [X], nitrates [X] mg/L, phosphates [X] mg/L
Site [C/name]: pH [X], nitrates [X] mg/L, phosphates [X] mg/L

Please:

Tell us which site looks healthiest and why
Describe what is changing as we move downstream
Suggest two possible reasons for the difference between the best and worst sites

Please explain at a level suitable for secondary school students (ages 11–16).

## 🦟 Template 3 — What animals can survive here?

Use when: students want to connect water-quality data to real wildlife.

A river site has these readings:

pH: [VALUE]
Nitrates: [VALUE] mg/L
Phosphates: [VALUE] mg/L

Which of these animals would you expect to find at this site, and which would struggle to survive? Please explain why for each one:

Mayfly larvae
Stonefly larvae
Water snails
Salmon or trout
Algae

Please write your answer at a level suitable for KS3/KS4 students.


---

## ✉️ Template 4 — Helping with a letter to a decision-maker

Use when: students are drafting letters to councillors, MPs, or water companies.

A secondary school student is writing a letter to [a local councillor / their MP / a water company] about river pollution.

The data they found:

Site near [location]: nitrates [VALUE] mg/L, phosphates [VALUE] mg/L
This is [describe: e.g. "three times higher than the healthy limit"]
The most likely cause is [e.g. "agricultural run-off from nearby fields"]

The student wants to:

Explain the problem clearly using the data
Say why it matters for wildlife and people
Ask the decision-maker to take one specific action

Please write a short example paragraph they could use or adapt — written in the voice of a confident secondary school student, formal but direct.


---

## 🌿 Template 5 — Action ideas

Use when: students want to do something but are not sure what.

A Year 9 class has found that their local river has high pollution levels (nitrates at [VALUE] mg/L, phosphates at [VALUE] mg/L).

They want to take action and are based at a secondary school.

Please suggest five realistic things they could do — from small (just at school) to bigger (reaching the community or decision-makers) — to help protect the river or raise awareness. Explain each idea in two sentences.


---

## 🤔 Template 6 — Critical thinking check

Use when: you want students to evaluate an AI answer they have already received.

You just told me: "[PASTE THE AI'S PREVIOUS ANSWER HERE]"

Now please:

Tell me one thing in that answer that you are very confident is correct
Tell me one thing that might be wrong or that you are less certain about
Suggest where I could look to check whether that part is accurate

This is one of the most important prompts in the toolkit. It models critical evaluation of AI output and builds AI literacy directly.

---

## 🌍 Template 7 — Connecting to global rivers

Use when: you want to broaden the lesson beyond the local river.

We have been studying pollution in a river in [UK / your country].

Are rivers in other parts of the world facing similar problems? Please give two examples of rivers in different countries that are affected by [agricultural run-off / plastic pollution / industrial waste], and describe what is being done to help them.

Please write at a level suitable for secondary school students (KS3/KS4).

## Tips for classroom use

**Prompt as a class first.** Before giving students free access to the AI, run a prompt together on the board. Model how to read the response critically.

**Save interesting responses.** If the AI says something surprising or that students want to investigate further, copy it. It becomes a research starting point.

**Name the limitations.** The AI does not know your specific river. It is answering from general scientific knowledge. This is a feature, not a bug — it teaches students to combine general knowledge with their own local evidence.

**Prompt revision is a writing skill.** Getting students to rewrite a weak prompt into a strong one is excellent English and AI literacy practice.
