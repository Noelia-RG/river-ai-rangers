## Background — Why Oxygen Matters

Fish and invertebrates need dissolved oxygen (DO) to breathe — just as we need oxygen in the air. Unlike pH or nitrates, dissolved oxygen can change rapidly — within hours — in response to temperature, algae, and pollution. It is one of the most sensitive indicators of river health.

**Healthy dissolved oxygen levels: above 8 mg/L** Below 5 mg/L: most fish begin to struggle Below 3 mg/L: only pollution-tolerant species survive Below 1 mg/L: the river is effectively dead
